# Refunds

Money going back out. Transcribed from `references/100626/` — this folder is the
**current state**, reproduced in Birdie components at tablet size, so that what
ships can be argued with precisely before anything is redesigned.

Like Rainchecks, this is organised by **journey** rather than by screen, because
a refund is not a screen. It is Order Lookup, a results list, a transaction, a
builder, a dialog and a banner, and the failures live in the joins between them.

## The path

| # | Story | Screen | What happens |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| 1 | **Find the order** | Order Lookup → Results | Search is scoped to one course and one day; refunds are rows with a minus sign |
| 2 | **The transaction** | Transaction Details | The receipt, six facts, and the only **REFUND** button in the app |
| 3 | **Create a refund** | Create a Refund | Steppers set the amount, checkboxes set the tenders, one sentence totals it |
| 4 | **The reason** | Dialog | "Why are you issuing this refund?" — free text, green OK, no Cancel |
| 5 | **The result** | Results + Transaction Details | A green band, and the refund as a brand-new order |
| 6 | **End to end** | All of it | The whole path, live — seven screens and two full-screen loads |

## The model underneath

**A refund is a new order, not a change to the old one.** 6521260 is the sale;
6521287 is its refund, two minutes later, carrying the returned lines at −1
apiece and its own negative payments. The two are never linked on screen — not in
the results list, not on either record.

Everything else follows from that. The results list cannot show a refunded order
as refunded, because the original is untouched. Transaction Details offers REFUND
on an order that has already been refunded in full, and on a refund itself.
Reconciling a day means matching equal and opposite amounts by eye.

## What the flow does not tell the operator

These are the findings, in the order they cost the most:

1. **The tender split is invisible until after the fact.** Refunding $42.34
   across Cash and a Gift Card puts $25.00 on one and $17.34 on the other. The
   operator checks two boxes and sees those numbers for the first time on the
   finished record, in step 5.
2. **Validation arrives on press, and says the wrong thing.** Nothing is said
   while a selection is made; REFUND with nothing checked produces *"Price of
   selected payments is less than total price to be refunded"* — true of an empty
   selection, but the actual problem is that no destination was chosen. It names
   no shortfall and blocks nothing visibly.
3. **Excluding an item means counting it to 0,** which leaves the line in place
   at full price. On the finished record that line is simply gone, so the record
   cannot distinguish "kept it" from "was never on the order".
4. **The reason prompt is not a confirmation.** It repeats no amount, no tender
   and no item count, offers no Cancel, and commits with a green button after the
   screen behind it used a red one. A mis-set stepper has nothing to catch it.
5. **Tax has no line to go back on.** Order 6516551 totalled $18.44 and charged
   $18.44, but its only item prices at $15.44, so the fullest refund the screen
   can build is $15.44. Worth settling what the missing $3.00 is before this
   screen is redrawn.
6. **The flow ends on a list.** Success is a green band naming no order, over the
   day's results; the refund has to be found by eye. Nothing is printed and
   nothing is offered to the guest.
