# Aug 24 — the raincheck nobody can find

Concepts, not decisions. Nothing here is wired into the prototype.

## The incident

> *"Employee says that'll be $100. Customer says I have a raincheck that covers
> this. Employee clicks raincheck payment type and searches customer name.
> **Nothing comes up.** Employee says 'I don't see it here.' Customer gets mad.
> Employee has to talk to the manager to look it up in buck and then they find
> out **customer used it at a different course a few weeks ago**."*

And the written requirement alongside it:

> *"Add a searchable raincheck listing, including filters like expired. Ability
> to see raincheck issuance date/course and any activities on that raincheck.
> This info should also be available in the raincheck payment result."*

## What actually goes wrong

`searchRainchecks` filters on `isRedeemable`, which excludes spent, voided **and**
expired credits. So a used raincheck does not rank low — **it does not exist**.

The operator gets an empty list and reads it as *"this customer has no
raincheck."* The true answer was *"they had one, they used it, here is where."*
Two different facts, one answer. That collapse is the whole incident, and the
manager step exists to undo it.

## The fact that had nowhere to live

**The model had no course.** Not on the credit, not on a redemption.

*"Used it at a different course"* is the load-bearing detail of the story, and
until Aug 24 there was nowhere to record it — so the terminal could say a credit
was empty but never *where* it went. Everything else in this folder is
presentation; this part is data, and it gates the rest.

`Raincheck.course` and `Redemption.course` now exist, because a credit cut at one
course and spent at another over several visits is the ordinary case in a
multi-course operation.

## The reframe

Weston framed this as a search problem. **The common case is an empty-state
problem**, and that is a better place to fix it.

The tender already opens on the cart customer's credits with no search. In the
incident the employee *searched* — which means the default view had already
failed them. If the default instead says:

> **No credits available.** Weston Senior has had 3 rainchecks. The most recent
> activity was 5/02/2026 at Falls Road — Twilight green fee.

…nobody types anything, nobody says *"I don't see it here"*, and the customer
hears a resolution instead of a denial. See **1 — The counter moment → Nothing
spendable**, which is the fix for the incident as described and involves no
search at all.

Search options only matter for the harder case: the credit is under another name.

## Three options for the harder case

The real fork is **where a credit's history lives when you are mid-sale**. Weston
named the tension himself — *"we could just tell the employee to go to customer
look up… but it would send them to another screen."*

| | Approach | Bets that | Costs |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **A** | One list, never empty. Spendable on top, the rest below a divider with a reason | The operator should never need a second screen or a second question | The densest results of the three |
| **B** | *Available* / *History* as sibling tabs, with a count badge | Keep the common path pristine; make the awkward one deliberate | The badge is the only thing preventing a repeat of the incident |
| **C** | The customer's record, opened **over** the sale | This history already exists and does not want rebuilding in a tender pane | Still a second surface; discoverability does the work |

All three share the same default and the same fix for the common case. They
differ only in the search path, so switching between them is a sideways move.

## On the performance worry

> *"My worry is if we include all exhausted rainchecks it will slow down the
> search for the more common use case of actually using a raincheck when there is
> one."*

Worth pushing back on, gently.

**Search is not the common case.** The tender opens on the cart customer's
credits without a search at all, so by the time anyone types, the default has
already failed them — and that is precisely the moment they want everything.

**The cost is not query time.** It is an indexed lookup on one customer either
way. The cost is visual noise, and noise is solved by **ranking, not omission**.
Spendable credits stay first on screen; nothing has to be hidden to keep them
there. Omission is what produced the empty list in the first place.

## What was already built

Weston's first proposal — rainchecks on Customer Lookup, current plus expired —
**already exists**: see **Record & register → 3**, which splits money owed from
money lapsed and opens a credit with every redemption on it. Worth knowing before
anyone rebuilds it. His second proposal, searching exhausted credits from the
payment screen, is the genuinely new part and is what **1 — The counter moment**
explores.

## What each screen answers

| Screen | Question |
| :-- | :-- |
| **1 — The counter moment** | *Can this customer pay with a raincheck, and if not, why not?* |
| **2 — The searchable listing** | *Find me this raincheck* — when the name is not matching |
| **3 — The payment result** | *What happened to it* — so next month's question is answered by the receipt |
| **Record & register → 3** | *What does this person have?* — when you already know who they are |

The listing and the record are **not** duplicates. The record assumes you have
identified the customer. The listing is for when you have not: a misspelling, a
spouse's booking, a company account, or a slip carrying nothing but an id.

## Still open

- **Is search scoped to the current course today?** If a customer's *unspent*
  credit from another course is also invisible, that is a worse bug than the used
  one — a live credit failing at the wrong terminal — and it should lead this
  work rather than follow it. **Nobody has checked the device.** This is the one
  genuine blocker.
- **Can an expired credit be honoured with a manager override?** The listing
  needs to know whether `expired` means *dead* or *ask someone*. It also decides
  whether the expired filter is a report or a worklist.
- **Should a course see every course's credits by default,** or its own first?
  Showing everything is what makes the cross-course case answerable; a shop may
  reasonably want its own numbers first.
- **Which of A, B or C** — and it is worth noting they are not exclusive. A's
  never-empty default could ship with C's overlay for the deep case.
