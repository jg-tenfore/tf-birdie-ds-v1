# Aug 20 — issuing the whole group at once

Two options, from one note. Concepts, not decisions, and nothing here is wired
into the prototype.

## The note

> *"It would be nice to set up all the rainchecks on one screen. So you would not
> need to click through that flow four separate times for a foursome.*
>
> *You would need to be able to deselect the ones you do not want to issue a
> raincheck for.*
>
> *I wonder if something like name of the player you are issuing a raincheck to
> on the left and on the right of each player there is a drop down to assign it
> to someone else for each player.*
>
> *Could you maybe come up with one or two options we could explore? The thought
> is just making it easy to issue the raincheck for the whole group at once."*

## What it lands on

The **Aug 12–13 concept** made a single issuance clear — which round, how much,
whose account, what has already happened — and then made you do it four times. It
even advertised the repetition: after each credit, selection hopped to the next
player still owed one. That loop was drawn as a feature, and it is the thing
being complained about.

That screen has been **retired**. It is no longer in the sidebar; the source
survives at `concepts/rainchecks/reservation-raincheck.tsx` as the readable
record of what it was.

The event these screens exist for is one event. It rains, the horn goes, a
foursome walks off the ninth together and arrives at the counter as a group. The
flow treats that as four transactions because a raincheck is cut **per round** —
true of the record, untrue of the task. Both options keep the record per round
and make the task per group.

Three requirements came with the note, and both options meet all three:

| # | Requirement | How both options answer it |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| 1 | Everyone on one screen | One list, one press, one commit |
| 2 | Deselect | A tick per row. Unticked rows grey but stay legible — you have to be able to see what you turned off |
| 3 | Reassign per player | The dropdown on the right, exactly as described |

## Where the options differ

One question, and only one: **the hole count**.

| | **Option A — Row per player** | **Option B — One stop for the group** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Hole count | On every row | Once, at the top |
| Group walks off together | Four identical adjustments | One |
| Group splits up | Nothing special — it is already per row | Tap that row's hole count to turn it into a stepper; the row marks itself `SET BY HAND` |
| Nine-hole booking in an eighteen-hole group | Needs no explanation | Needs a banner and a per-row sentence |
| Row height | Taller — stepper on every row | Shorter — rows carry only what differs |
| Bets that | Groups stop at **different** holes | Groups stop at the **same** hole |

Everything else is identical on purpose. Same ticks, same dropdown, same amber
tint on a reassigned row, same band across the bottom, same action bar. The
comparison is only worth running if one variable moves.

**There is a third design neither file draws:** A, with B's group control at the
top as a bulk-set. It would probably win. It is left out so the two bets stay
legible against each other — and so the question underneath gets answered rather
than designed around.

### The question underneath

**How often does a group stop at different holes?** Nobody in either transcript
has said, and it decides this. One call to a pro shop settles it:

- *Almost always together* → Option B, and A's per-row steppers are four controls
  earning their space once a season.
- *Routinely split* → Option A, and B's group number is a default operators fight.
- *Both, regularly* → build the hybrid, and the group control is a convenience
  rather than the model.

## What to look at

| Story | Why |
| :-- | :-- |
| **A → Rained-out foursome** | The batch working. Untick a row, reassign one, then issue all of them in one press |
| **B → Rained-out foursome** | Drag the group slider and watch four different prices move together off one number |
| **A → Two of them finished** | Requirement 2, on its own |
| **A → One credit to somebody else** | Requirement 3, on its own — Weston's visiting-buddy case as one dropdown instead of a second trip through the flow |
| **B → One of them played on** | The exception path — tap a row's hole count — and the `SET BY HAND` mark that stops a later group change dragging it back |
| **A / B → A nine among the eighteens** | The same fixture in both. A needs no explanation; B needs a banner. This is the clearest single argument between them |
| **A / B → One round already credited** | A round cannot be rainchecked twice, and neither the shipping screen nor the earlier concept can show that state |
| **End to end → Option A / Option B** | The same trip through each: tee sheet → the group screen → where the credits land. The only place the batch's claim — one visit, not four — can actually be checked |

## The trip, not just the screen

**End to end** runs each option from the tee sheet. Both use the same harness and
differ only in the middle step, so anything that feels different between them is
the hole-count question and nothing else.

Three things live there that neither option story can show:

- **The entry point has not moved.** Step one is the shipping detail screen
  itself — the same component **Flows → Rainchecks → 1 — Booking with a
  raincheck** renders — with its buttons made live. Weston asked for the red
  RAINCHECK button to stay exactly where it is. What changes is where it *goes*:
  tapping it on one player now opens the whole group, which is his own *"anytime
  you hit rain check, it just takes you there"* extended from one round to four.
- **Coming back.** After the batch, every credited player carries a bolt and no
  longer offers RAINCHECK. The single-issue flow reaches that state after four
  round trips.
- **What the dropdown is actually for.** The landing groups new credits by the
  **account** holding them rather than by round, so a foursome that put two
  credits on one name shows up as one account with two. That is the only thing
  reassignment produces, and it is invisible on the issue screen itself.

## Same ground rule as before

> *"we can just change that in the existing system and use this as a good
> breeding ground — this is a new flow that we're trying with the old design
> system, but a new flow."*

No new tokens, no new colours, no new button styles. Same navy band, same green,
same MD2 surfaces, 48dp touch floor throughout. What changes is the arrangement
and what the screen says out loud.

Two things are borrowed from the earlier concept and worth naming as deliberate:
the **amber tint** on a reassigned row is the only new signal, and it exists
because scanning four rows for the odd one out should be a colour rather than a
reading task. And the action bar states the outcome — **Issue 4 rainchecks ·
$206.18** — because four credits leaving the course is not something to commit
behind a bare verb.

## Still open

- **How often groups split.** The question above. Everything else follows from it.
- **Should the dropdown reach outside the group?** Both options list only the
  people on the reservation. Weston's guest case is always inside the group; a
  league organiser collecting four credits under one account may not be. There is
  no *search for another customer* entry on purpose, so the omission gets noticed
  rather than assumed.
- **Does a batch need a confirmation step?** Four credits in one press is a
  bigger money action than one, and neither option has a dialog. The band and the
  labelled button are the review. That may not be enough.
- **What the nine-hole clamp should do.** Crediting Tom Watson from the last hole
  of his own round leaves him $3.38 against the others' $33.33. That may be right
  — he finished his nine — or it may be a case for dropping him off the group
  number rather than clamping him to it.
- **Does a twosome get this screen at all,** or fall back to the single-issue one?
- **Undo.** Voiding exists per credit — see **Voiding a credit** in either option
  — but there is no *undo that batch*, and a batch is where a wrong hole count
  does four times the damage.
