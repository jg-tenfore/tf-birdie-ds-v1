# Rainchecks

A raincheck is a credit for a round the weather cut short. It is created on the
tee sheet, lives on the customer's record, and is spent at the register — three
screens, days apart, on one object.

Everywhere else in this Storybook is organised by screen, mirroring the
reference folders one for one. This folder is organised by **journey**, because
the interesting failures in rainchecks are not inside any single screen. They are
in the joins: a credit created against a booking whose name does not match any
customer, a balance a counter cannot find, a credit spent twice. The same
components appear here and under **App Screens** — this is a second way in, not a
second implementation.

## The loop

| # | Story | Screen | What happens |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| 1 | **Booking with a raincheck** | Tee time detail | A paid round gains a red **Raincheck** action |
| 2 | **Create raincheck** | Raincheck | Holes played sets the credit; **CREATE RAINCHECK** files it |
| 3 | **The credit on the record** | Customer record | It appears under Gift Cards, with a running balance |
| 4 | **Redeem at the register** | Checkout, RAIN tender | Look it up by id, name or email; pick it; apply it |
| 5 | **Order complete** | Order Complete | The receipt prints a `Rain Check` payment line |

## The pricing rule

The credit is the **unplayed** share of the round, at the price actually paid:

```
credit = roundPrice × (totalHoles − holesPlayed) ÷ totalHoles
```

An 18-hole round at $100 abandoned after 5 holes returns 13/18 — `$72.22 (72%)`.
Two consequences worth being deliberate about:

- **0 holes played returns everything.** A group that never teed off gets the
  round back in full, which is right, and means the create screen's default state
  is the most generous one it can produce.
- **There is no option for 18.** A completed round has nothing to give back, so
  the radios stop at 17. On a 9-hole round they stop at 8.

The percentage is rounded to a whole number while the amount is not, so `72%`
sits beside `$72.22`. Those two numbers match only because the round cost exactly
$100 — a $66 round shows `$47.67 (72%)`.

## Where the code lives

| Piece | File |
| :-- | :-- |
| Model, pricing, lookup | `src/data/rainchecks.ts` |
| Ledger of credits | `src/data/customers.json` owners, `state.rainchecks` at runtime |
| Create screen | `src/components/screens/tee-sheet/raincheck-form.tsx` |
| Record section | `RainChecksTable` in `src/components/screens/operations/customer-search-panel.tsx` |
| RAIN tender | `src/components/screens/checkout/checkout-panes.tsx` |
| Order Complete | `src/components/screens/checkout/order-complete.tsx` |

The ledger is one list. The tee sheet appends to it and the register spends from
it, which is what makes a credit created in the prototype findable at the till a
moment later. Modelling it the other way — a `raincheck: true` flag on the round
plus a discount at checkout — is the shape this started in, and it cannot answer
"how much am I owed?" from any screen.

## Open questions

- **Partial redemption.** The device can take a credit that does not cover the
  ticket and leave a balance owing. The prototype refuses and names the
  shortfall, because there is no split-tender model behind it. Worth a decision
  before this ships.
- **Expiry.** Every credit carries an expiry date and nothing enforces it — an
  expired raincheck is still offered as a chip at the register.
- **Who can issue one.** Any operator can cut a credit for any amount up to the
  full round. There is no approval step and no audit beyond the reservation's own
  history.
