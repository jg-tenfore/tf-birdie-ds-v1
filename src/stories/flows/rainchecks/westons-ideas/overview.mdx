# Weston's ideas

Concepts, not decisions. Everything in this folder comes from two conversations —
the **Aug 12** raincheck walkthrough and the **Aug 13** follow-up — and exists so
the ideas can be argued about as screens rather than as sentences in a
transcript.

Nothing here is wired into the prototype. The shipping screens are unchanged and
live under **Flows → Rainchecks** steps 1–5.

## The ground rule

> *"we can just change that in the existing system and use this as a good breeding
> ground — this is a new flow that we're trying with the old design system, but a
> new flow."*

So these concepts introduce **no new tokens, no new colours and no new button
styles**. Same navy bars, same green, same MD2 surfaces. What changes is the
arrangement, the defaults, and what the screen says out loud.

## What he was actually complaining about

> *"the issuing's the part where I'm like, man, it just feels weird… you can get
> it done, but it could be more clear."*

The shipping issuance screen asks one question — how many holes — and leaves
three others implicit:

| Question | Where the shipping screen answers it |
| :-- | :-- |
| Which round is being refunded? | Implied by which player's button you tapped |
| How much comes back? | The radios. This part works |
| Whose account receives it? | The unlabelled chips at the bottom |
| Has this round already been credited? | Nowhere |

The third is the one that bit. Weston could not state with confidence what the
chips did — *"I think what that's doing is where I issue it for you because
you're out of town, but to my name. And that's just not clear really
whatsoever."* If the person who owns the product hedges on it, no operator is
getting it right.

## What the concepts change

They are numbered to match the five core steps above, so each concept pairs with
the screen it would replace. **1** and **5** have no concept on purpose: the
booking is the entry point Weston asked to leave alone — *"people are used to it,
I do want to change it in v2"* — and Order Complete is unchanged.

| Core step | Concept |
| :-- | :-- |
| 1 — Booking with a raincheck | *unchanged, deliberately* |
| 2 — Create raincheck | **2 — Create raincheck** |
| 3 — The credit on the record | **3 — The credit on the record** |
| 4 — Redeem at the register | **4 — Redeem at the register** |
| 5 — Order complete | *unchanged* |
| — | **Create one, end to end**, which spans 1 to 4 |

**2 — Create raincheck.** All four questions asked out loud: a list of
positions with their fee structure and their raincheck state, the holes control
with the amount *below* it rather than above, a labelled recipient picker, and a
plain sentence stating the whole outcome before you commit. Already-credited
rounds are greyed and cannot be selected.

**3 — The credit on the record.** The record's raincheck rows open. Tapping one brings up
the credit in full — the round behind it, the money, every draw against it, the
void record if it has one — and offers the void from there.

That placement is deliberate. The argument happens at the counter with the
customer standing there ("that was supposed to be on my account"), and the person
who can fix it is looking at this record; sending them to find the reservation
first is a detour through a screen they do not need. Void is two taps here rather
than one, because the record is a browsing screen and this is the only thing on
it that destroys value.

**4 — Redeem at the register.** The tender opens on the ticket
customer's credits instead of an empty search field. Each credit names the round
it came from — the seven o'clock on the twentieth, not "51381" — and says
whether it clears the ticket *before* you apply it rather than after.

It also shows the three states a raincheck can be in, rather than only the one
that can be spent:

| State | How it reads |
| :-- | :-- |
| Untouched | Full balance, tappable, says whether it clears the ticket |
| Partly spent | What is left leads; the draw that took the rest sits underneath |
| Used up | Greyed, `USED`, not tappable, with where every dollar went |
| Expired | Greyed, `EXPIRED` in orange, with what was still on it when it lapsed |
| Voided | Greyed, `VOIDED` in red, naming who cancelled it, when, and why |

Both dead states come from the pro shop rather than from us. MCG's feedback was
about the awkward counter moment when a customer produces a slip the terminal
insists does not exist — which happens both when the credit has been spent and
when it has lapsed. Showing them is the fix; **how long expired credits are kept,
and whether one can still be honoured, is not settled.**

The used row is the one worth arguing about. The shipping lookup filters
spent-out credits away before they reach the screen, which looks tidy and costs
real time: a customer who is certain they have a raincheck gets "no results", and
the operator cannot say *you spent it on the 25th of April on a glove and two
sleeves*. That sentence ends the conversation; "nothing found" starts a longer
one. It also means a customer with nothing left and a customer who never had one
are indistinguishable at the counter.

Search sits **above** the list and opens **full screen**. Two reasons, and the
second is the one that settled it. Finding a credit on somebody else's account is
a different job from picking one of this customer's two, and a 45%-wide column
under a tender strip is the wrong room for it. And on glass, **the keyboard eats
a field low on the screen** — which is also why moving the search bar to the
bottom only makes sense if every payment type moves with it. The modal borrows
the app's own lookup shape: one centred, underlined field, the same as Customer
Search. The trade is that the ticket leaves the screen while you look, so the
modal's bar restates what is owed.

**Applied credits land in the order summary, and come back off.** Each one is its
own line under Total Payments — `Rain Check 38204 · −$14.76 · $0.00 left on it` —
with an X. Two credits can settle one ticket between them, and the band reads
"Paid in full" when they do. The shipping pane has a Total Payments row and
nothing behind it, so a part-payment applied by mistake means voiding the sale.

The same gap runs the other way on the issue screen: an already-credited round
now carries what happened to that credit afterwards, so issuance and redemption
meet on one row instead of living on two screens a month apart.

**Create one, end to end.** The other three show screens; this one shows the
*object*. Start on the tee time, tap the red RAINCHECK where it has always been,
credit a round, and watch the credit land on the customer's record and at the
till as the same thing — carrying the reservation and the round it was cut from.

That is Weston's point made walkable rather than argued: *"it's not tied to a
customer, it's tied to that reservation you've booked."* **Issue another** runs it
again against the same booking, which is how a rained-off foursome is actually
handled — four credits, one at a time, off one reservation.

## Voiding a credit

Nothing in the flow could take a raincheck back. **BACK** cancels before you
commit; after that there was no undo on any screen, in a system where a round
can only be rainchecked once — so a credit cut for the wrong player left that
round locked as well as the money misplaced. The transcripts never raised it,
which may mean the policy is deliberate; it is not stated anywhere either way.

**Void, not delete.** A raincheck is a financial record and deleting one takes
its audit trail with it. Voiding leaves the credit in the ledger marked `VOIDED`,
keeps it on the customer's record, and releases the round so a correct one can be
cut.

- Reachable two ways: from the issued row on the reservation, and from the
  credit's detail sheet on the customer's record. Same rules, same reason list.
- **A reason is required**, from a fixed list. The correction worth counting is
  *issued to the wrong player*, and that is the number that would justify
  redesigning this screen. A free-text box would be left blank.
- **Blocked once any of it has been spent.** The row says so rather than hiding
  the control: at that point the money has left and it is a refund question, not
  a correction.
- Both the issue and the void land in the reservation's history.

Two things are guesses and should be decided rather than inherited from this
concept: **whether any operator can void or it needs a manager**, and **whether
there is a time limit** — same shift, same day, or forever.

The record separates what is owed from what has lapsed, for the same reason:
`$190.88` owed and `$49.67` expired are different facts, and folding the second
into the first would have a counter read out a number the terminal will not
honour.

## The finding worth keeping

Justin, who had never used the flow:

> *"I literally thought that was like delete customer. Because I never looked at a
> big red button — I always think red buttons is delete."*

And Weston, one day earlier, on why the button exists at all:

> *"you can't delete it, you don't want to cancel it — it's already paid for.
> That's the only way to do something with it."*

A refund affordance wearing a destructive colour, and it is the only action a
paid reservation has. Worth carrying into the v2 conversation even though the
button is deliberately not moving now.

## Still open

- **Does the recipient picker match the real behaviour?** The concept assumes
  Weston's reading — the chips choose the account, not the round. He said "I
  think". Confirm against the device before this is more than a concept.
- **Should a raincheck be spendable on anything, or only on a tee time?** It is
  built from a green fee and a cart fee. It is currently a general tender.
- **Partial redemption.** The device leaves a balance owing; the prototype
  refuses. The concept's "$31.68 would still be owed" line assumes the device's
  behaviour is the one to keep.
