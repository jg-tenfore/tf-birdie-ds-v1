# Bay Sheet — Oct 30

Everything in this category answers one document:
**[PRD — Bay Sheet Enhancements](https://linear.app/tenfore/document/prd-bay-sheet-enhancements-d75cb6a50d85)**,
written by Weston on Oct 30.

> *"Staff need one screen to see every bay's schedule, book and edit
> reservations, and charge customers, the same way they run the tee sheet
> today."*

It is built in step with **[Prototype V2](/prototype/v2.html)** — the same
components run in both, so nothing here is a picture of something that does not
work.

## Nov 1 — the dialogs, rebuilt

Justin's notes on the Oct 30 draft. Four changes, all of them to the three
dialogs:

1. **The footer is cancel and save, and nothing else.** It was carrying up to
   five keys, four of them red, which made the most irreversible things on the
   screen the loudest and left the footer unreadable at a glance.
2. **The destructive actions moved into the body** — each its own button,
   flexed to fill the width, under a heading that says what they do. See
   **[DangerActions](?path=/story/bay-sheet-oct-30-components--danger)**.
3. **Every question is a bordered field with its label inside it**, from the
   reference Justin sent. The bay is a dropdown; how long, party size and
   repeat each take the full width. See
   **[FieldShell](?path=/story/bay-sheet-oct-30-components--shell)**.
4. **The dialog is much taller** and the note is six rows, because requirement
   4 puts notes on the sheet where everyone reads them all day.

One thing worth arguing with: the footer now says **CANCEL** beside a body
button that says **CANCEL BOOKING**. Those are two very different acts sharing
a word. The footer wording is what was asked for, so it is what is built —
but *CLOSE* beside *CANCEL BOOKING* would be unambiguous, and the change is one
line.

## Every dialog has an address

A review that says *"open the details modal on the standing Tuesday lesson"* is
a sentence and three taps. These are links, which is the difference between a
comment somebody can act on and one they have to reconstruct.

Times are `HHMM` on a 24-hour clock.

| Link | What opens |
| :-- | :-- |
| [`#/baysheet`](/prototype/v2.html#/baysheet) | The sheet |
| [`#/baysheet/reservation/r-7`](/prototype/v2.html#/baysheet/reservation/r-7) | Justin's standing Tuesday lesson — a repeating booking, so all three destructive actions |
| [`#/baysheet/reservation/r-2`](/prototype/v2.html#/baysheet/reservation/r-2) | Maya's 9:00, where a tab can be opened |
| [`#/baysheet/book/teal/1100`](/prototype/v2.html#/baysheet/book/teal/1100) | A new booking, Teal Bay at 11:00 AM |
| [`#/baysheet/book/blue/1100?customer=c-6`](/prototype/v2.html#/baysheet/book/blue/1100?customer=c-6) | The overlap that is refused |
| [`#/baysheet/book/magenta/1500?customer=c-1`](/prototype/v2.html#/baysheet/book/magenta/1500?customer=c-1) | The daily limit, and its override |

that does not exist opens the sheet rather than an error.

## The parts list

**[Components](?path=/story/bay-sheet-oct-30-components--sheet)** has one story
per component, twenty-two of them, and reading down the sidebar is the whole
surface area of the feature: six things that draw the day, fifteen that ask a

## Where the shipping sheet stops

The bay sheet already exists. It draws six bays over four hours and knows four
things about a reservation: who, how long, how many, and what it costs. The PRD
asks it to know nine more — checked in, paid, whether a tab is open, the note,
whether it repeats, whether it was cancelled or no-showed, which punch card
paid for it, and whether booking it puts the golfer over their daily limit.

## Two decisions worth arguing with

**The two guards are drawn differently on purpose.** An overlap is red and has
no way past it — requirement 10 says prevent it. The daily limit is amber and
has a switch in it — requirement 18 says staff can override it. One is physics,
the other is policy, and a screen that drew them alike would teach staff to
ignore both. See **[ConflictWarning](?path=/story/bay-sheet-oct-30-components--conflict)** and
**[LimitWarning](?path=/story/bay-sheet-oct-30-components--limit)**.

**The chip reads its own height.** Seven things have to fit in a column about
150px wide, so only the name and the duration are words and everything else is
a mark. A two-hour booking has room for its note as text; a thirty-minute one
does not, and shows a glyph instead. The legend above the sheet teaches the
marks, because they cannot teach themselves.

**The sheet scrolls sideways rather than squeezing.** Nov 1: fifteen bays, of
which eight fit a 1280 tablet. 150px is where a chip stops reading, so past
that the sheet gets wider than the screen and the clock and the bay names ride
`position: sticky` instead. The **[filter row](?path=/story/bay-sheet-oct-30-components--filter)**
is the other half of the answer — choose the six bays you are running tonight
and nothing scrolls at all. Its marks narrow rather than widen, and it keeps a
count on the right, because a filtered sheet and a quiet day are the same
picture.

## The layout is decided — Nov 2

**Option A, the calendar grid, was picked unanimously** in the review with
Weston and Cody. It is now simply
**[1 — The day](?path=/story/bay-sheet-oct-30-screens--sheet)**. Minutes are
pixels, a chip's height *is* its duration, and you read down a column.

**[Option B](?path=/story/bay-sheet-oct-30-screens--sheet-rows)** drew the same
day the way the shipping tee sheet draws its own — one white card per half
hour, the clock on the left, the bays across it. It is kept as the record of
what was weighed and marked *not chosen*. Cody: *"I want to get away from that
paradigm that B has."* What they were buying was less visual noise: *"I like
the flags for the colouring instead of the whole freaking tile being the
colour."*

That leaves the bay sheet and the tee sheet looking different for a while,
which the review accepted on purpose — *"that's a pill we just have to swallow
for a while, because if we don't start moving pieces over bit by bit we're
never going to change."* The plan is to introduce the new elements into the tee
sheet gradually, starting with its detail view becoming a slide-over.

**What came out of that review is in
[Cody to Review › Nov 2 — Bay Sheet](?path=/docs/cody-to-review-what-changed-nov-2--docs)**,
with the three things still open called out.

## Weston's open questions, and what this build assumed

The PRD ends with ten questions. None are answered here — these are the
working assumptions, each one cheap to reverse.

| His question | What this build does |
| :-- | :-- |
| What device, and how many bays fit without scrolling? | **Eight of fifteen** at 1280 landscape; the day scrolls down and the rest of the bays scroll across. The filter is how a house with fifteen bays gets a sheet that fits. See also **[9 — Zoomed out](?path=/story/bay-sheet-oct-30-screens--zoomed-out)**, which fits all fourteen hours and shows what that costs |
| Do repeats need an end date or a number of occurrences? | Neither yet. The picker says the cadence and the dialog says the question is open |
| One confirmation email per series, or one per occurrence? | Not modelled |
| Does the daily limit count reservations or minutes? Across all bays? Who can override? | **Reservations**, across all bays, anyone can override |
| What if a punch card only covers part of the fee? | The shortfall is shown and still charged — never silently absorbed |
| Paid, unpaid, or open tab while a tab is open? | **Its own state.** Paid and unpaid are the only two the shipping sheet can say, and the answer is plainly a third |
| Can staff create a customer, or book a walk-in with no profile? | Neither door is wired |
| Is there a cancellation fee? Are payments refunded and punches returned? | Not modelled |
| Can a block cover existing reservations? Should it have a label? | **No** to the first, by running the same overlap check; **yes** to the second — a block cannot be saved without a reason |
| Which parts need backend work? | Out of scope here, but the four that clearly do: recurrence, overlap checks, the daily limit, and the no-show status |

## About the data

The bays, the fee idea and the party/duration fields come from the reference
device. **Everything else is invented** — the fifteen bay names, the customers,
the rates, the punch cards, the daily limit of two, and the seeded day.

That day is built to carry every state at once rather than to look plausible:
two checked in, one with an open tab, one paid, one with a note long enough to
crowd its chip, two weekly series, a cancellation and a no-show that have given
their time back, and two blocks.
