# What changed — Nov 3

**Everything here came out of the Nov 3 check-in with Weston.** It is shorter
than the Nov 2 page and it matters more, because two of the five items
**replace** things Nov 2 asked you to build rather than adding to them.

Nothing below needs a call. Two items name an open question, and both are

---

## Read this first if you read nothing else

**The three membership statuses never existed.** Nov 2 built the ribbon on
Simulator Member / Member / Public, because those were the three Weston named
out loud. On Nov 3 he showed where memberships actually come from:

> *"There could be an infinite number of memberships. So we don't have
> simulator memberships or public, like it's all just, you can create
> different membership types… And they all have a colour associated with
> them."*

A course creates as many plans as it likes, and **picks each plan's colour on
the plan itself**. The tee sheet already draws that colour as a bar under each
cell — which is the pattern the ribbon was borrowed from in the first place.

So the colour is **data**, not a design decision, and `WJ-169` is answered: the
ribbon reads the membership record. The screens are rebuilt around that, and
the three invented colours are gone.

## 1. The ribbon, rebuilt

Four of Weston's own plans are in the sample day, by name. Three things follow
that are worth knowing before you build it:

**The colour is drawn exactly as the course picked it.** No darkening, no
contrast floor. The point of the ribbon was that it matches the tee sheet's
bar and the membership form — *"that's the pattern we've adopted on the tee
sheet"* — and a colour we corrected would be a third version of the same
membership.

**That has a visible cost, and it is on the screen on purpose.** Weston's own
*Super Membership* is yellow. Maya Okafor's 9am booking carries it, and at 4px
on white it barely reads. That is not a bug to file; it is the consequence of
the decision above, put where you can see it. If it turns out to matter, the
fix belongs in the membership form's colour picker rather than here.

**The legend reports the day, not the catalogue.** It lists the memberships
actually on this sheet and adds *No membership* only when somebody is a
walk-up. A fixed legend was possible with three statuses. With his test
course's dozen plans it would wrap to several rows of memberships nobody
booked.

Holding no membership is the one uncoloured case: a neutral grey that claims
only *not a member*, duller than anything a colour picker is likely to produce.

## 2. Bay colour, removed

Weston, looking at the block dialog:

> *"They're not always a colour… we don't have a designated colour, and some
> people use colour names, others just use like the Jack Nicklaus Bay and the
> Tiger Woods Bay."*

A bay is an activity resource on the backend and a resource has no colour
field, so there was nothing to read — the sheet was inventing it. **It is out
of all five places it was drawn**: the block dialog's bay picker, the
reservation dialog's bay picker, the sheet's column headings, the bay filter,
and the rows variant's headings.

Two consequences:

- **A bay is identified by its name.** The sample bays are now a mix — ten
  colour names, an East and a West Porch Sim, a Patio Sim, and two named after
  people — which is the spread a real course list has and the one the screen
  has to survive.
- **The column headings needed something else.** The swatch was doing real
  work at fifteen bays wide: it let your eye find a column and track down it.
  Every other column now gets a barely-there neutral wash, which cannot be
  mistaken for a fact about the bay and works at any number of columns.

`color` is still on the bay record, unread, because he wants it eventually —
*"I wouldn't mind having that… we just don't have it right now."* Grep it and
you will find every place that has to change when it comes back.

**The not-chosen Option B still shows bay colours.** That is deliberate: it is
the record of what was weighed on Oct 30 and nothing since has been applied to
it.

## 3. Fees, rebuilt as conditional pricing

The Nov 2 page asked you for a middle row in a rate table. **Withdraw that
question** — the table was the wrong shape. A fee is a record:

> *"It says from 12 to 9, I want my gold members… get this fee, $50. And then
> maybe I'd create a gold morning. Everything's the same except it's $75."*

Five of the six in `bay-data.ts` are copied off his fee screen with their real
prices and priorities; the sixth is the Gold Morning he described creating,
because a membership whose rate changes at midday is the point of the feature.

| Fee | Price | When | For | Priority |
| :-- | --: | :-- | :-- | --: |
| Sim Hour Sun–Thurs | $100 | all day, Sun–Thu | anyone | 1 |
| Sim Hour Fri–Sat Morning | $150 | to 12pm, Fri–Sat | anyone | 2 |
| Sim Hour Fri–Sat Evening | $200 | from 12pm, Fri–Sat | anyone | 3 |
| Gold Afternoon | $50 | 12:00pm–9:01pm | Gold Punch | 4 |
| Gold Morning | $75 | to 12pm | Gold Punch | 5 |
| Default Rate | $120 | all day | anyone | 6 |

The design consequence is small and good: **a fee has a name**, so an order
line says `Gold Afternoon` instead of a rate we made up. Open the 560 panel
story and you will see it resolve — a 1pm gold member books at $50.

**Price stays off the chips.** Weston on whether the sheet should show it:
*"I don't know if we need to display it on here. We can see kind of what
feedback we get."* So nothing was added.

### The open question in this one

**Does a membership-specific fee outrank a general one, or does the priority
number win outright?** Read the column at face value and `Gold Afternoon`
(priority 4) never applies on a Tuesday, because `Sim Hour Sun–Thurs`
(priority 1) matches the same hours — which contradicts Weston describing it
as the fee his gold members get. The prototype assumes **most specific first,
priority breaks ties**. If the backend instead expects the operator to order
the list correctly, say so; it is four lines either way. It is the open
question on [WJ-238](https://linear.app/tenfore/issue/WJ-238).

### The other thing this exposed

Fees depend on the weekday, and **the sheet is one day wide with no date on
it**. The sample day is fixed to a Tuesday so the general rate resolves. The
shipping screen knows its own date, so this is a prototype limitation rather
than a design question — but it is worth knowing that the day's identity is
now load-bearing for pricing in a way it was not before.

## 4. Full-card, and why the text moved

When there were three colours I owned, each had a darkened twin that carried
white text at 4.5:1. A course choosing its own colours breaks that outright:
white on Weston's yellow is unreadable.

The card now keeps the colour exactly, like the ribbon does, and **moves the
ink instead** — black or white per chip, by luminance. Put Maya's yellow card
beside the amber ones in story B and it is the same treatment with different
ink.

Still **for spec, not for now**, and still carrying Cody's unanswered
question about where a course would set the preference.

## 5. Multi-bay blocking, confirmed

> *"I really like it, the multi-select."*

Unchanged, except that the ticked swatches went with the bay colours. The
closed field now names the bays while they fit and counts them after — `Red
Bay, Teal Bay`, then `4 bays`. Naming them matters more here than anywhere
else, because this is the one field that can block four bays in one press and
`3 bays` is not something you can check before you hit save.
