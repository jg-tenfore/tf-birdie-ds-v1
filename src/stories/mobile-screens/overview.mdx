# Mobile Screens

The same Birdie POS, laid out for a phone. From `references/090426/`.

**402 × 797**, portrait, Android — the only portrait viewport in the system, and
deliberately scoped to this category so a landscape screen cannot be drawn
against it by accident.

## This is a re-layout, not a restyle

Every colour, radius, weight and casing comes from `app-replica-tokens` exactly
as the landscape screens use them. **Nothing here introduces a token, a colour,
or a component the design system did not already have.** The slate app bar is
the same slate. The green PAY is the same green. The seat bands import
`seatBandColors` from the tablet's own order panel rather than redeclaring them.

What changes is arrangement, and only where 402px forces it.

## The four moves

The landscape screens are built from three fixed regions — a **390px order
panel**, a **content pane** beside it, and a **bottom action bar** of four to six
equal buttons. At 402px all three break at once: 390 of 402 is the whole screen,
side-by-side is impossible, and six buttons in a row gives each one 67px.

| Landscape | Mobile | Why |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Order panel **beside** content | Two **bottom-nav destinations** | Both stay one tap away; neither is cut to a sliver |
| Product **tile grid** | A **list**, thumbnail + name + price | Six across becomes two; a tile is mostly padding |
| 4–6 **action buttons in a row** | One full-width primary, secondaries stacked above | `PRINT COMBINED SEATS` does not fit in 67px |
| Centred **modal dialog** | A **full screen**, or a **bottom sheet** for a short menu | A 640px dialog does not fit in 402px |

Two smaller rules follow from those:

- **Anchored menus become bottom sheets.** At this width a menu anchored to the
  row it acts on covers the row it acts on. Same options, same order, same red
  on the destructive one — it just comes up from where a thumb already is.
- **The attached customer pins above the nav.** *Whose order this is* is the one
  fact an operator must never navigate to check, and the item count rides on it
  so the order stays legible from the menu side.

## The system chrome is drawn on purpose

`MobileFrame` renders the Android status bar (24dp) and the three-button
navigation bar (48dp), because the references do and because together they take
**72 of the 797**. Every screen here is built against the ~725dp that actually
remains. A design checked against the full height is a design that ships 72dp
too tall.

## Where the references stop

The screenshots in `references/090426/` cover the register: sign-in, the drawer,
Quick Order, Tabs, Tables, Table Order, Customer Lookup, the full tender flow,
Open Food, item edit, Order Complete — six of App Screens' eighteen folders.

**All eighteen are now built.** The other twelve are extrapolated from the four
rules above rather than transcribed from a mock, and each one names the trade it
made in its own docs.

| | App Screens | Mobile Screens |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Folders | 18 | **18** |
| Stories | 72 | **79** |

The count is higher because narrowing sometimes splits one tablet screen into
two — Checkout's ticket and tender, Customer Search's results and record — and
because a few screens gained a state the tablet never needed, like Quick Order's
empty order once the panel stopped being permanently visible.

## What the phone gives up

Six screens lose a real capability. They are listed here rather than left for
someone to discover, because each is a product decision hiding inside a layout
problem.

| Screen | Lost | Why it cannot be recovered at 402px |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **2-teesheet** — Multi & Back 9 | Comparing courses, or the front and back nine, **at a glance** | Three columns of four positions is 32px per position. The data survives behind a switcher; the side-by-side read does not, and that read is the whole reason those views exist |
| **3-coursheet** | *"Is anything free at 7:20?"* as **one row** across six facilities | Six columns at 402px are 67px each. That question now costs up to six taps. Nothing single-column recovers it — if it turns out to be the phone's main job, the answer is **a different screen** (availability keyed by time), not a smaller grid |
| **4-baysheet** | **Duration as shape** — a 90-minute block becomes the words "90 min" | A calendar encodes length as height. A list has one row height. Partly rescued: the `All bays` tab keeps *"6 of 6 open"* per half hour, so **how many** survives; **which** still costs a tap |
| **7-tables** | **Choosing which table you are serving** | A floor plan is spatial. Shrink the room and every table is too small to hit; crop it and you hide half. The phone assumes you arrived with a table in hand |
| **10-tablechart** | **Placing** tables in a room | The phone lists a room's tables and can add one. It does not position them — that is a sit-down task on a counter terminal. The screen prints this rather than hiding it |
| **11-customerSearch** — profile | **Awarded and Spent** on a raincheck row | The credit table is 7 columns. Balance is what a counter is asked for, so it survives and the other two do not — which means **a partly-spent credit now looks like a small one**. The ledger stays a terminal job |

The pattern is consistent: **spatial and multi-axis views do not narrow.** They
survive as one-axis-at-a-time with a switcher, which keeps every fact and loses
the comparison.

Two of these are worth arguing with rather than accepting:

- **3-coursheet.** If a phone's actual job on a court sheet is "what is free
  right now", then a facility switcher is answering the wrong question well. The
  fix is a screen this project has not drawn.
- **11-customerSearch.** Dropping Awarded and Spent is the only place in this
  category where **data disappears** rather than moving. Everything else here is
  a lost *view*; this is a lost *fact*, and it deserves a second look before
  anyone builds it.

## Reading a screen against its landscape original

Folder names mirror `App Screens` exactly, so the two categories can be opened
side by side. That is the whole point of the naming, and every story here names
the trade it made in its own docs.
